# Threat Model Documents — SPARQL 1.2 Query Language

**Subject:** `spec/index.html` — SPARQL 1.2 Query Language, Editor's Draft
**Reviewed at:** commit `7f707ff` (2026-10-01)
**Status:** Not a W3C publication; carries no normative weight; does not represent Working Group consensus.

The threat analysis for the SPARQL 1.2 Query Language is split into three documents, divided by what each one is *for* rather than by subject matter. The split exists because the three have different destinations: the first is intended for the specification itself, the other two as related work.

---

## The documents

### [Threat Model](threat-model.md) — description

*Intended for the specification.*

What the threats are. For each of 34 threats: the mechanism by which the language gives rise to it, quoted against the draft; its impact; and its severity in each of five deployment profiles. Also the system model and trust boundaries, the risk matrix, and STRIDE coverage.

**Deliberately contains no countermeasures.** It describes and does not prescribe, so that it can stand as analysis on its own and read naturally as specification prose. It is self-contained and does not depend on the other two documents.

`threat-model.md` · `threat-model.html`

### [Specification Actions](threat-model-spec-actions.md) — action, for the Working Group

What the Working Group could change in the draft: four recommendations, `R-1` to `R-4`. These include making explicit that a service may impose resource limits and refuse queries, draft content for the `TODO` privacy section, and fifteen suggested additions to the security section.

`threat-model-spec-actions.md` · `threat-model-spec-actions.html`

### [Implementation Advice](threat-model-advice.md) — action, for implementers and deployers

What to do about the threats outside the specification: a six-layer defence architecture, per-threat countermeasures for 33 of the 34 threats, the residual risks that remain once those are in place, a threat-to-specification-section map, and three working checklists for implementers, deployers and application developers.

`threat-model-advice.md` · `threat-model-advice.html`

## How the three fit together

Each document is numbered from 1 in its own right. What is shared between them is the **identifiers**, not the section numbers:

- `SQ-NET-03`, `SQ-CONF-01` and the other 32 threat identifiers mean the same thing in all three documents, and are defined in the threat model.
- `R-1` to `R-4` are the specification recommendations, defined in Specification Actions.
- `P1` to `P5` are the deployment profiles and `A1` to `A8` the assets, both defined in the threat model's system model.

Dependencies run one way. Specification Actions and Implementation Advice both cite the threat model; the threat model cites neither of them. A reader who wants only the analysis needs one document; a reader deciding what to do needs two.
